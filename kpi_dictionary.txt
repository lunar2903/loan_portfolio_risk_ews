Credit Risk:
    The risk that borrower fails to meet their financial obligations
And this is the central problem this prooject is trying to analyze.

DPD: Days past due
    DPD tells us how late customer is on repayment 

|   DPD | Bucket                      |
| ----- | --------------------------- |
|     0 | Current                     |
|  1–30 | Early delinquency           |
| 31–60 | Moderate delinquency        |
| 61–90 | Severe delinquency          |
|   90+ | Very severe / NPA territory |


But how does the system know someone is risky?
    We look for signals:
    1. Payment delay
    2. Repeated late payments
    3. EMI Bounce
    4. Increasing debt
    5. Credit deterioration
    6. Utilization
    7. DPD migration

Account-level vs exposure-level thinking:
    10% of accounts are delinquent.

    But suppose those 100 loans represent only:

    ₹5 crore

    out of a total portfolio of:

    ₹500 crore

    Then exposure-based delinquency is:

    1%

    Now reverse it.

    Suppose only 20 loans are delinquent, but they represent:

    ₹100 crore

NPA = Non-Performing Asset:
    It refers to assets that has stopped generating income for bank

Roll-rate analysis
    This is used to calculate how accounts have migrated between states of DPD.
    This tells us whether the portfolio is curing or deteriorating.

Delinquency
    The borrower has failed to make a required payment on time.

SMA — Special Mention Account:
    SMA classifications identify loans showing stress before they become NPAs.

Default:
    The borrower has failed to meet a contractual obligation according to the defined default framework.
    "Failed to meet a contractual obligation": Missed a scheduled payment, 
    failed to maintain required collateral, or broke another promise agreed to in the contract.

Cure:
    The borrower became delinquent but returned to current status.

Write-off:
    A write-off is an accounting action that removes the value of an asset from a company's books because it is no longer collectable, usable, or recoverable.

Segmentation:
    Segmentation means dividing the loan portfolio into meaningful groups so that we can compare risk between them.

Average Ticket Size (ATS):
    It tells us approximately how large the average loan is.

    Total exposure = ₹100 Cr
    Number of loans = 10,000

    Average Ticket Size
    = ₹100 Cr / 10,000
    = ₹1 lakh

DTI — Debt-to-Income Ratio:
    How large are the borrower's debt obligations relative to their income?

Exposure:
    How much money does the bank currently have lent out?

NPA Ratio:
    NPA ratio measures the proportion of the loan portfolio classified as non-performing under the applicable framework.

Gross NPA:
    This broadly looks at the total amount of gross non-performing assets relative to the relevant gross advances/exposure base.

Provision:
    Money recognized/set aside to absorb anticipated credit losses.

The analyst's hierarchy:

    When analyzing portfolio risk, I want you to develop this habit:

    First ask:

    How big is the segment?

    Exposure
    Then:

    How risky is it?

    Delinquency / NPA / Default
    Then:

    How much risk does it contribute?

    Risky exposure
    Then:

    Is the risk getting better or worse?

    Trend / Roll rate / Vintage
    Finally:

    What might happen next?

    Early warning / PD / Expected Loss

    This sequence is basically the backbone of our entire project.
